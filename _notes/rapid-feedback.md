> This is part of the [[Principles for Software Engineering]] compilation.

We want **code feedback** ("does it work?") - this comes in the form of testing. A unit test is immediate, tangible feedback that a piece of code does what I think it does.

We want **product feedback** ("does it solve the problem well?") - this comes in the form of user testing, product testing, rapid deployments to production to continuously get feedback on new features.

We want **interpersonal feedback** ("are we doing this well?") - this comes in the form of retrospectives and other touchpoints to consider team health and adjustments to be made.

In all cases, we want ***rapid*** feedback, which is why we write tests as we go, so we immediately know if the software works - we don't wait until days have passed implementing a huge feature to start testing it. It's why we deploy to production as often as possible - ideally one or more times a week - we don't wait weeks or months between building a feature and discovering if it really solves our users' problems or not. And we seek timely feedback from our teammates - if something I'm doing is hurting our team, I want to know *now*, when I am best suited to make a change.
