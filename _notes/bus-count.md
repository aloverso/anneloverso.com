Your **bus count** is the number of people on your team who would have to get hit by a bus before the project stalls and is unable to continue.

The expression has been criticized for its morbidity (rightly so?) and a popular alternative phrase is **lottery count** (the number of people who would have to win the lottery and quit their jobs because they have more money than they could ever need).

Bus count is used as a measure of risk - particularly risk due to knowledge silos.

> **You never want to have a bus count of 1.**

## Why it matters

With a low bus count, your team is highly susceptible to risk due to individual missing links. What if a key person goes on a three-week vacation, and they're the only ones who (know how to deploy to prod) (know the database credentials) (can read their poorly-maintained spaghetti code) (understands the complexities of the legacy system)?

A resilient team [[shared context / shared responsibility|shares knowledge]] across team members so that any individual is not solely responsible for success, and their loss would not be catastrophic to productivity.

## Management & Psychological Safety

This is impossible without a management structure that embraces psychological safety and encourages collaboration over individual accomplishments. If an individual is evaluated on the basis of their individual importance and contribution, it incentivizes people to build knowledge domains and stake a hostile claim over their territory to prove their importance to management. This lowers a team bus count and is overall disastrous to long-term product health.
